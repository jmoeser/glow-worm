# Recurring payments

Read this before changing confirm, skip, pay, or `paid_from`. `AGENTS.md` only points here. Contract stamping is summarized here and specified in `docs/contracts-and-payment-methods.md`. Overflow debit rules depend on month-end settlement in `docs/monthly-processing.md`.

## Recurring payments

`app/services/recurring_payments.py`. Each bill has `paid_from` (`bills_fund` by default, `budget`, or `sinking_fund` with `paid_from_fund_id`). Each bill also has `importance` (`essential` for keep paying, `optional` for safe to cancel, or null for not set yet), plus optional `cancel_note` and `if_stopped_note` (500 characters, blank stored as null). The mark is a label. Confirm, skip, pay, auto-pay, the forecast, bills cover, and the recommended deposit ignore it. A safe-to-cancel payment is still paid until someone cancels it with the provider or deactivates it. The Bills page splits the monthly averages already shown (Bills-fund monthly plus other recurring) into keep paying, safe to cancel, and not marked. Those figures include every active payment, whatever it is paid from. The foreign amount is not included. Only Bills-fund rows feed the forecast, the recommended amount, the cover banner, and the scheduler. Weekly and fortnightly step by 7 and 14 days. Every 2 years (`every_2_years`, label Every 2 Years) steps with `add_months(current, 24)`: 31 Jan 2026 becomes 31 Jan 2028, and 29 Feb 2028 becomes 28 Feb 2030, then 28 Feb 2032. Confirm, skip, pay, and auto-pay all step with that same `advance_due_date`. An unknown frequency raises `UnknownFrequencyError`, which is not a `ValueError`. Confirm, skip, pay, and cover still treat `ValueError` as a bad date. A person who confirms, skips, or pays a bill with an unknown frequency gets that frequency named back, and the cursor does not move. Payday distribution rolls the month back instead of posting a guessed amount. Auto-pay and the three-minute boot catch-up do not: one unknown frequency fails only that bill. There is no 30-day step. `iter_bill_due_dates` stops after `end_date`; confirm, skip, and the scheduler do too. Payments from a budget or another fund, and variable Bills-fund bills, wait in Due to confirm. Confirm is dated on the due date. A budget confirm raises `spent_amount`. It debits overflow only when that due month's `household_months.settlement_outcome` is `surplus`, `shortfall`, or `zero` and the stored fund still exists and is not soft-deleted. The debit is the confirm amount, from that stored fund, not from today's overflow setting. Old months with no sweep record, `no_overflow`, and `unresolved` record the payment and do not debit. Skip writes no transaction. `GET /api/bills/due`, `POST /api/bills/{id}/confirm`, and `POST /api/bills/{id}/skip` share the service with the web UI, MCP, and CLI. Confirm, pay, and scheduler auto-pay copy `bill.contract_id` onto the payment (see Contracts). The overflow clawback withdrawal does not.

## Occurrence transitions

`transition_occurrence` is the only settler for confirm, skip, manual pay, and auto-pay. It flushes and does not commit. HTML, JSON, and MCP commit once after it returns. Auto-pay is the only caller that commits per bill, after its savepoint releases. One bill's failure, including `UnknownFrequencyError`, rolls back that savepoint only. The 06:00 job and the boot catch-up both call `process_due_bills`. Confirm calls `ensure_backfill` before the transition; that commit does not contain the cursor move, the payment, or the clawback. Skip, manual pay, and auto-pay do not call it.

The occurrence is `next_due_date` when the compare-and-set runs. A payment stores it as `occurrence_due_date`. Pay, Confirm, and auto-pay are the only writers of that column. Skip writes no row. A hand-entered transaction does not settle an occurrence, whether or not it names a bill. A one-off Bills fund expense with no bill id is a normal transaction: it debits the fund, stays fully editable and deletable, and does not move any cursor or stop auto-pay. Recording the bank debit that way, and leaving the cursor, means the startup catch-up will also debit the Bills fund.

Deleting either row of a linked pair (the payment or the clawback that points at it with `adjustment_for_transaction_id`) reverses both and deletes both. `next_due_date` and `last_auto_settled_on` stay put. A partner that is already gone is a one-row reverse. Rows with both new columns null, including old take-backs, stay a one-row delete. They are not paired by the description.

An HTML save of a bill is one conditional update of the edited columns, `WHERE next_due_date` is the date the form loaded (`next_due_date_was`). A missing or invalid date saves nothing. JSON `PUT` and MCP `update_bill` require `expected_next_due_date` when `next_due_date` is sent. Omitting the date leaves the cursor alone and still saves the other fields. `last_auto_settled_on` is not client-writable.

`last_auto_settled_on` is the auto-pay daily slot only. Manual pay does not read or write it. A fixed bill that is several periods overdue can be debited twice on a day the person also presses Pay: once for the date they paid, once for the next date auto-pay takes. That second debit is money that is still due.

The late-confirm clawback is unchanged. It debits the stored settlement fund by the confirm amount when the outcome is `surplus`, `shortfall`, or `zero` and that fund still exists. The description stays `Taken back for {name} ({Month Year} {category})`. The overlap described in `household_months.py` is not closed: a confirm that overlaps settlement can still miss the debit, and a concurrent bill can still save a stale fund balance over settlement. The Bills fund is not the overflow fund.
